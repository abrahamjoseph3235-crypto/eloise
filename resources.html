When you need urgent help

If you or another person is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency service or go to the nearest emergency department.

Eloise is not an emergency service. We will publish verified, country-specific crisis contacts before launch. Please do not rely on this prototype for emergency contact details.

Practical guides

Preparing for a first session

You do not need to know exactly what to say. You can share what prompted you to seek support, ask about confidentiality, and agree on goals together.

  • Ask about the provider's qualifications and approach
  • Discuss fees, cancellations and session format
  • Ask what happens if you need a referral

Understanding privacy

Before sharing sensitive information, ask how it is stored, who can access it, when it may be shared and how you can request a copy or deletion.

  • Read the privacy notice and consent terms
  • Use a device and connection you trust
  • Do not send clinical details through general support forms

Support between sessions

Ask your provider what to do between appointments and what contact they can offer. Routine messaging is not a substitute for emergency support.

  • Agree on realistic response times
  • Keep useful resources in one place
  • Know where to seek urgent help

Screening is not diagnosis

Questionnaires can help structure a conversation, but a score alone cannot establish a diagnosis or determine the best treatment. A qualified professional can discuss what the result means in context.

Only use assessment tools when their purpose, consent, scoring, interpretation and clinical oversight have been properly reviewed.

Choosing a provider

Check the person's credentials, registration where applicable, experience with your concerns, language, fees and availability. It is reasonable to ask questions or seek another provider if the fit does not feel right.
